Anatomy

A hat is a handful of parts, each one a decision. These are the parts, and where they show up in Joseph’s current hats.

  1. Felt

    01

    The body of the hat. Weight decides how it holds a shape: dress weight is lighter and finer; western weight is heavier and stiffer.

    In the current hats: natural and granite (dress weight), pecan (western weight), whiskey, camel.

  2. Band

    02

    Sits where crown meets brim. The first thing people notice, and the easiest place to make a hat personal.

    In the current hats: twine wrapped twice (Artemis); rust suede (Cerelia); an indigo denim accent (Ahab); a tonal ribbon (Oris).

  3. Bow & keeper

    03

    The knot of ribbon on the band, and the small loop that holds it. A detail measured in millimetres.

    In the current hats: a two-tone bow and keeper (Artemis); two-tone ribbons (Ahab).

  4. Binding

    04

    A ribbon folded over the brim edge. It protects the edge and draws a line around the hat; leaving part of it off is a choice.

    In the current hats: a partial brim binding (Oris).

  5. Adornment

    05

    Charms, feathers, beadwork. Small objects with private meanings, added last.

    In the current hats: gold sailboat, moon, leaf and flower charms; a turkey feather; beadwork.

  6. Embroidery

    06

    Thread worked into the felt itself. The most labour, the least room for error.

    In the current hats: floral and script embroidery (Cerelia).
