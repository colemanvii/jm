## 1. What DropOut Companies actually is

The research corrected an assumption in the brief. **DropOut Companies is not a design studio.** It is a **Nashville consumer-products house**, founded in 2023 by Connor Blakley and Josh Franko, that builds packaged-food brands alongside athletes and creators.

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Nashville-based consumer products house, founded 2023 by Connor Blakley and Josh Franko | Food Industry Executive; PR Newswire (Dec 2025) |
| Brands: **Jams** (frozen high-protein PB&J; Walmart July 2025, Target Nov 2025) and **Bronco** (frozen breakfast bagels; Target Jan 2026) | CNBC; Refrigerated & Frozen Foods; Food Business News |
| Develops "better for you" products "alongside iconic talent"; athlete and creator investors include Alex Morgan and Caleb Williams | LinkedIn; press releases |
| Jams developed with NFL Players, Inc., with player input on flavours, ingredients **and packaging** | Food Business News |
| Approach described by operating partner Stephen Ellsworth (co-founder of Poppi): take familiar foods and rebuild them with better ingredients; Blakley "culture obsessed" | Food Industry Executive |
| A design firm, Fra Mauro, built a Webflow landing page and investor portal for "consumer talent-led holdco" The DropOut Companies (2023) | fra-mauro.com |

**Limits of the visual research.** The working environment's network policy blocked the brands' own websites, the Fra Mauro case study page and image sources. **I could not look at DropOut's packaging, art direction, typography or digital execution**, and no source names their packaging designers. The principles below therefore come from their **strategy, naming, positioning and operating model**, which are documented, not from their visual style. That also guards against imitation. To complete the visual research, allow `jams.com`, `eatbronco.com` (or the brands' actual domains), `fra-mauro.com` and `instagram.com`.

## 2. Principles, and what each means for Joseph Martin

| DropOut principle (evidence) | Translated for Joseph Martin | Where it shows in Study D |
|---|---|---|
| **Rebuild a familiar American staple for now** (PB&J → Jams; breakfast sandwich → Bronco) | The American hat rebuilt for now: Western shapes and dress fedoras without costume or nostalgia | Contemporary type and photography, with no Western motifs anywhere |
| **Talent at the centre**: brands built alongside people, not around products | **Joseph is the talent.** The people who wear the hats are the cast | Full-bleed portrait and name as the opening; "In Joseph's words"; a People section |
| **A house of brands**: one company, distinct identities | A small house: **Hats, Drawings, People, Music City Driving Club**, with a shared system and distinct sub-identities | The four-world index; the club gets its own colour, typeface and mark |
| **Plain, countable claims on the front** (10 g protein, no seed oils) | Price, weeks and waitlist said plainly, with no adjectives | Hat reel prices, "Have one made" table, "Commissions by waitlist" in the hero |
| **Short vernacular names with character** (Jams, Bronco) | The hats already do this (Oris, Ahab, Artemis); keep section names one plain word | Navigation: Hats · Drawings · People · Driving Club |
| **Culture first**: the founder is "culture obsessed" | Nashville culture through people, driving and drawing, not guitars or neon | Club identity drawn from American road-sign vernacular |
| **Nashville** | Shared city. Be specific about the place: Villa Place, Nashville, Tennessee | Hero foot, Visit section, club sign |

What is deliberately **not** carried over: mass-retail shouting, product-claim badges, celebrity endorsement. Nothing confirms any celebrity relationship for Joseph, and none is implied.

## 3. The concept

**A person, then a world.** The site stops being a shop with a story and becomes a portrait of a maker with a shop inside it.

1. **Joseph:** a full-bleed portrait with his name set very large in Instrument Serif, roman "Joseph" against italic "Martin". The only other words are *Hatmaker. Nashville, Tennessee.* and *Commissions by waitlist →*.
2. **The index:** the four worlds as an oversized typographic list that is both statement and navigation. Each row carries one useful fact: *From $595*, *Originals*, *Wearers*, *MCDC*.
3. **Hats:** a swipeable reel of what's in the shop now, priced and linked to the live Shopify product pages, ending on a custom card (*Yours, any shape. $1,395*). Commerce arrives **after** the editorial impression and never interrupts it.
4. **In Joseph's words:** a working portrait beside a quote slot that stays empty until Joseph supplies the line.
5. **Drawings:** a wall of his originals, shown at full size with the paper edge. In this system **his drawings replace all decoration**: labels, boxes, the club plaque, the site's marks.
6. **People:** customers photographed in their own lives, named only with permission. Swipeable on phone.
7. **Music City Driving Club:** a sub-brand band in road-sign green. The club's name is set like an American guide sign, mixed case in **Overpass**, a descendant of the FHWA Highway Gothic signage letterforms, white on green inside a rounded white border. It has a dash-plaque mark and a road centre line. Copy is limited to what is known: *a driving club is taking shape around the shop*, plus a sign-up.
8. **Have one made / Visit:** price, time and waitlist; address and directions.

**The authored device: the plate.** One small outlined plate (Overpass capitals, 4 px radius) recurs across the site. It joins the two physical objects at the heart of this world: the size tag inside a hat and the dash plaque of a driving club. It is used sparingly, for labels only.

## 4. Visual system

| Role | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Instrument Serif** (roman + italic) | Condensed, high-contrast, contemporary. It can carry the name at 248 px and a hat name at 34 px. Its pairing of roman and italic gives an authored rhythm |
| Text and UI | **Instrument Sans** | Same family voice, neutral at small sizes |
| Club and plates | **Overpass** | A descendant of the FHWA Highway Gothic letterforms used on US road signs: American vernacular without costume |
| Colour | Cool paper `#F3F3EF`, ink `#141413`, photo ground `#1B1A18`, whiskey felt `#8E5A2C`, route green `#0E5B3B` | Neutral ground so photography leads; felt colour for hats; one strong sub-brand colour for the club |

All faces are SIL Open Font License, self-hosted, with licences in `assets/fonts/`.

## 5. Honesty notes

- **Photography and drawings:** none exist in the repo. Every frame is a labelled placeholder with a shot or selection brief. The concept depends on real photography and Joseph's own drawings. Without them it is a well-made frame, not the finished work.
- **Music City Driving Club:** no public information was found. The name, sign treatment and plaque are an **identity study**. Its purpose, membership, routes and schedule are not stated because they are not known.
- **The Ahab price** is still "to confirm" ($1,675 vs $1,395).
- **No quotation from Joseph** is invented. The quote slot says so.

## 6. Critique (as creative director)

**Stronger than A–C at:** putting Joseph first; giving the drawings and the club a place without clutter; making commerce feel like part of a world rather than a storefront. On first impression it is the most culturally specific of the four. The club band is the most memorable single moment.

**Weaknesses:**
- The opening depends on a portrait that does not exist yet. With a weak photograph, the big-name-over-portrait opener is a familiar move. The photograph has to be exceptional: art-direct it, don't take it as found.
- Until real images arrive, the long run of dark frames (words, people) makes the middle of the page heavy.
- The road centre line is the one detail closest to gimmick. Keep it as the single motion moment, or cut it.
- B's felt colour system, the strongest idea in A–C, appears here only in the hat reel. A final direction could bring B's felt-switch into the Hats section.

**Possible next step (not a decision):** combine D's structure (Joseph, the world, the club) with B's felt system inside the Hats section.
