# Joseph Martin — Creative Direction
**Status:** Approved governing direction · 23 November 2026  
**Applies to:** All Joseph Martin digital redesigns, concepts, product pages, editorial pages, commissions, and collaborations, including MCDC.

## The brief
**An American hatmaker with the refinement of a global luxury house.**

Joseph's ambition is a highly refined brand. The website must communicate that ambition before a visitor reads copy. This is not a generic Western ecommerce site, a nostalgia project, a novelty interaction playground, or a collection of unrelated experiments.

**Creative shorthand:** Range Rover's restraint; the precision of a watchmaker; American craftsmanship and humanity. These are references to qualities, **not** invitations to imitate a specific brand's identity.

## Approved visual anchor
**Study B: The Object**, as reviewed on 23 November 2026, is the primary visual benchmark for the refresh. Preserve its underlying design intelligence, not necessarily the literal layout on every page.

Specific approved qualities:
- Restrained, widely tracked uppercase JOSEPH MARTIN wordmark; quiet luxury / automotive-level composure.
- Deep warm whiskey-brown field against clean ivory linework and type.
- Oversized, authoritative product naming contrasted with fine technical illustration.
- Annotated hat anatomy and clear construction details; the product is worthy of study.
- Disciplined navigation (HATS / ANATOMY / COMMISSION).
- Material/color swatches integrated as precise, functional controls.
- Careful rules, alignment, whitespace, proportions and hierarchy.
- A human, tactile warmth without kitsch.

This is a direction endorsement, not blanket approval of all copy, data, pricing, responsiveness, imagery, or ecommerce functions in any prototype. Treat unverified product details as placeholders, not facts.

## Five non-negotiables

### 1. Quiet authority
Luxury is earned through hierarchy, spacing, photography, materials, finish and precise execution—not through decorative signifiers or claims that the brand is “premium.” Fewer and better visual decisions.

### 2. The object comes first
Hats are the central subject. Use exceptional product imagery, articulate specifications, considered technical drawings and thoughtful product interaction. A drawing must describe something real; do not invent construction details to fill a page.

### 3. Craft without costume
American roots, real making, real people. No distressed fake-heritage graphics, stock cowboy clichés, faux patina, saloon references or unnecessary rustic styling. Avoid performative “old money” tropes too. Refined does not mean sterile.

### 4. An editorial world
Photography and writing should reveal Joseph, his workshop, customers, objects, process and meaningful collaborations. MCDC belongs as an intentionally art-directed part of Joseph's world—not a competing microsite, unrelated style exercise or forced front-page spectacle.

### 5. Discipline in every interaction
Purposeful, fast, intuitive, accessible. Motion only when it increases understanding or creates a subtle material sensation. Strong desktop **and** mobile composition. No scroll-jacking, animation for its own sake, gratuitous 3D, gimmicky cursors, or surprise navigation.

## Working visual system
- **Type:** Considered, modern, confident. Uppercase tracking where appropriate; strong display type balanced with quiet utilitarian information. The approved wordmark's feel matters more than chasing a fashionable font. Avoid novelty Western type or arbitrary font pairings.
- **Color:** Warm whiskey / tobacco, ivory, material neutrals. Product colors can extend this palette. Introduce a new accent only if justified by an actual object or editorial story; do not make every page a different “theme.”
- **Composition:** Editorial and architectural: grids, crisp edges, fine rules, substantial whitespace and controlled scale. No card-heavy SaaS aesthetics, generic templates, gradients, gratuitous shadows or decorative clutter.
- **Images:** Honest documentary photography, real people and workshop details, exceptional hat/object studies. Avoid generic stock, synthetic-looking imagery and filler. If assets are missing, state what photography is needed rather than silently substituting.
- **Illustration:** Clean, precise, product-informed line drawings and annotations. Treat technical illustration as a possible brand signature, not a mandatory decoration everywhere.
- **Copy:** Specific, unforced, succinct. Make actual facts and decisions visible. Avoid luxury buzzwords, vague poetry, invented heritage claims, and filler statements.

## Site architecture — flexible, coherent
- **The Object:** hats, material, construction, buying/commission information.
- **The Maker:** Joseph, tools, making, philosophy and documentary portraiture.
- **The World:** real people, collaborations, culture and editorial projects such as Music City Driving Club.
These are content lenses, **not** a mandate to add three menu items or create three microsites. Architecture follows genuine content and practical customer journeys.

## Execution rules for Claude and collaborators
1. Read this document and the repository's `CLAUDE.md` **before** suggesting or implementing visual changes.
2. Start with Study B's approved language. Refine and extend it; do not repeatedly reset the art direction.
3. For new page concepts, describe the page's job and explain its continuity with Study B in a few sentences **before** coding.
4. Present **one disciplined recommendation by default**, not a scattershot suite of wildly different aesthetics. Multiple variants only when specifically requested or when a real design decision remains unresolved.
5. Reuse the existing visual vocabulary and real assets first. Never add filler photography, fake product information, invented reviews, pseudo-heritage or unapproved pricing.
6. Prefer the smallest elegant change over an elaborate redesign. Do not disturb an approved element merely to make the next iteration look novel.
7. Review desktop and phone renders, typographic hierarchy, technical accuracy, contrast, readability, interaction states and accessibility before proposing sign-off.
8. Clearly distinguish **approved direction**, **proposed execution**, and **unknown factual content**.
9. Aesthetic experiments may live in clearly labeled studies, but must not replace the approved direction or be deployed as the primary experience without explicit approval.
10. When in doubt, reduce. Then improve what remains.

## Rejection test
Do not ship or present a design if it feels like:
- A trendy AI-generated landing page or generic luxury Shopify template.
- A theme-park Western brand, historical reenactment or manufactured heritage.
- A flashy creative agency portfolio overshadowing the hats.
- A disconnected visual concept with no relationship to Study B.
- An impressive desktop mockup with a compromised phone layout.
- A page padded with invented details, filler copy, stock/synthetic imagery or irrelevant features.

## Sign-off test
Before sharing, ask:
1. Does it feel **highly refined** at first glance?
2. Would it belong to the **same house** as Study B?
3. Is the object or real human story unmistakably central?
4. Does every element have a purpose and accurate provenance?
5. Is the mobile version as considered as desktop?
6. Is it quieter, clearer and more assured than the prior iteration?

**North star:** The product is handmade. The presentation is precise. The personality is unmistakable.
