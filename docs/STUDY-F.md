## Phase 1: what the renders show

The judgements below come from the existing desktop and phone renders in `screenshots/`, viewed side by side, and not from the stated intentions in each study's own notes.

**A note on the evidence.** In the original `e-house-desktop-full.png`, the *Martin Survives!* and *Don't Call It Luck!* posters render as blank white boxes. The cause was the render tool, not the design: it never scrolled, so lazy-loaded images were never requested, and it served `.webp` without a content type. The tool is fixed, and E has been re-rendered under the same conditions as F in `screenshots/compare/`, where the posters appear. The original E screenshots are left untouched.

### A — The Portrait

- **Strongest visual moment:** the commission chapter on near-black. "Any colour, any shape, any detail. Made once, for one head." sits above a four-column table of price, price, time and place, followed by four numbered steps. It is the most persuasive selling in any study.
- **Most original idea:** treating the site as a monograph. Hats are catalogued like plates in a catalogue raisonné, with plate number, name, felt and price.
- **Biggest weakness:** without photography it is a run of grey and black rectangles. The full desktop render is about 11,000 px tall and roughly 70% of it is empty plates. The centred title page is dignified but has no tension, and it is the layout most likely to resemble other heritage brands.
- **What survives:** the commission chapter (structure and copy), and roman-numeral chapters set as a quiet system rather than as ornament.

### B — The Object

- **Strongest visual moment:** the first screen. A whole field of whiskey felt with a white line drawing of a hat, annotated like a technical plate. It is the only first screen across A–E that looks finished without a photograph.
- **Most original idea:** **the felt is the colour system.** Choosing a felt re-colours the page and changes the hat. The palette is Joseph's materials rather than one chosen by a designer, so nobody else can own it.
- **Biggest weakness:** Joseph is absent, so there is no intimacy. Archivo condensed caps (ORIS, ANY COLOUR. ANY SHAPE. $1,395.) push towards streetwear and fight the delicacy of the work. The drawing is generic and stiff.
- **What survives:** felt as the only colour; the annotated drawing as the hat's stand-in until photographs exist; the felt switcher; the anatomy vocabulary (band, binding, adornment, embroidery) tied to specific hats.

### C — The Living Archive

- **Strongest visual moment:** the "Have a hat made for you" close, with a calm serif statement beside a mono ledger table. In the grid, the black "6–8 weeks" note tile among the felts.
- **Most original idea:** accession numbers (JM·H·003, JM·M·007). Missing content becomes part of the record ("To photograph", "Open question"), so the site is honest about being in progress.
- **Biggest weakness:** on desktop it reads as a mood board. There are 25 entries of different sizes, about a third of them empty frames, and the hats compete with swatches and notes. A buyer has to work to find a hat.
- **What survives:** the ledger line as a site-wide caption device; accession numbers; open questions shown as a designed gap; a *small, edited* filterable archive in place of the front door.

### D — The World

- **Strongest visual moment:** "Joseph *Martin*" in Instrument Serif at 250 px, roman then italic, set across the bottom of the first screen. It has personality, it is contemporary, and it does not lean on Western costume.
- **Most original idea:** the typographic index (Hats, Drawings, People, Driving Club, each set at about 150 px), which presents the brand as a world rather than as a shop.
- **Biggest weakness:** most of the page is placeholder. The hero is a near-empty radial gradient, the drawings wall is five blank frames, and People is three black boxes. The green road-sign club identity is a second brand language bolted onto the page.
- **What survives:** the name set in roman and italic. Everything else is superseded by E.

### E — The House

- **Strongest visual moment:** the real Double Decker Arts Festival poster beside red-ink Cowboy George on the Drawings wall. It is the first time the site shows genuine work with its own voice and humour. The Music City Driving Club band on cream stock, with the orange lockup and George, comes a close second.
- **Most original idea:** Joseph Martin as a house with extensions. The club is "printed on its own stock" as a sub-brand, with a view-transition hand-off to its own page.
- **Biggest weakness:** on the first screen, about 80% of the desktop viewport is a dark empty field (a placeholder over a gradient) with a shot brief in the corner. The hats arrive third, as a carousel of flat colour cards with no drawing, so the object has less presence than in B. The index lists the four worlds at equal size, which puts Driving Club on the same level as Hats. Rounded pills and 4 px plate radii soften a system that elsewhere is exacting. The People section is three black boxes with "Name" placeholders.
- **What survives:** the type pairing and the name; real posters and drawings; the club as a printed insert; the chapter-by-chapter editorial pace; "Have one made / Visit".

## Phase 2: the synthesis

### One sentence

**Joseph first, then the hat in its felt, then the hands, then the world around the work, kept deliberately smaller.**

### What F takes, and what it refuses

| From | Taken | Refused |
|---|---|---|
| E | Instrument Serif, the name in roman then italic; the editorial chapter rhythm; real posters and drawings; the club printed on its own stock | Equal billing for the four worlds; the full-bleed dark hero; rounded pills; the People section with no people |
| B | Felt as the only colour; the annotated hat drawing; the felt switcher; anatomy vocabulary tied to each hat | Archivo condensed caps; the generic boxy crown |
| C | The ledger line and accession numbers; designed gaps; a filterable archive with view transitions | The 25-tile mood board; giving notes and hats equal weight |
| A | The commission chapter on ink, with its table and four steps; roman-numeral chapters; the frontispiece portrait facing the name | Libre Caslon, which would make a third serif; the centred title page; plates alternating left and right |

### The language

- **Three faces, three jobs.** Instrument Serif carries the name, the hat names and the chapter titles. Instrument Sans is for reading. DM Mono is for the ledger: numbers, specifications, status. No other faces are used.
- **One colour system.** Paper and ink. The felts (Natural, Granite, Whiskey, Pecan) are the only colour on the hat pages. Red ink (`#A3221A`, sampled from the drawings) appears only where something is missing or still to confirm, so the drawings' own red doubles as the colour of honesty. The club keeps its cream stock and signal orange, inside its own frame.
- **One device: the ledger line.** Every object on the site has a hairline and a mono caption carrying its number, its name and its status (JM·P·001 · Joseph Martin, Villa Place · TO PHOTOGRAPH). This is what makes five pages read as one house, and it is also what makes the gaps legible.
- **Rhythm.** Five chapters on the homepage, I–V, each opened by the same head: a hairline, a numeral, a serif title and a short note. The background alternates paper, felt, paper, a darker paper and ink, so the page breathes in material.
- **Restraint.** Square corners, hairlines and no gradients. Motion is limited to rises on load, the line drawing on the felt stage, felt cross-fades and view transitions in the archive, and all of it is off under reduced motion.

### Hierarchy (the brief's order)

1. **Joseph:** the name at 250 px facing a frontispiece portrait (A's composition at E's scale), with the facts and three ways to buy under the name.
2. **The hats:** a full-bleed felt chapter. Each hat is drawn with the details its listing actually names (Oris has the feather, the beadwork, the leaf charm and the partial binding). Changing the felt re-colours the chapter and redraws the hat.
3. **The maker:** a statement, the work photograph and the four commission steps (the last three marked "process to confirm").
4. **Around the work:** two real artworks and the club card, **smaller and later** than the hats, on a darker paper. The note says so: "Supporting, never in front of the hats."
5. **Commission:** A's chapter, on ink.

## Why F is better

1. **It resolves E's central contradiction.** E says Joseph is the centre and then gives most of its first screen to an empty rectangle. F gives that space to the thing that exists now, his name set with conviction, and keeps the portrait at a scale where a placeholder reads as intent rather than as absence.
2. **The hat becomes an object again.** In E, a hat is a coloured rectangle in a carousel. In F, each hat is drawn with the details its listing names and shown in its own felt, and the page changes colour with it. That is B's strongest idea, now inside E's world.
3. **One language instead of five.** F replaces E's mixed signals (Overpass plates, pill buttons, a green palette left over from D, a fourth typeface) with three faces, one colour logic and one caption device that runs from the homepage to the archive dialog.
4. **The world is supporting, as the brief asks.** The drawings and the club are still there and still real, but they come after the hats and the maker, at a smaller scale.
5. **It holds up beyond the homepage.** A collection page, a product page, a maker page and an archive all work in the same system, which is the real test of an identity.

## Where F still falls short

Stated plainly, because Joseph deserves the same honesty the site shows him.

1. **The first screen is still half placeholder.** The composition is designed so the portrait can drop in, but until it does, the most important image on the site is a black rectangle. No layout can fix that.
2. **One generic hat drawing does all the work.** All four hats share one silhouette and vary only in their details. A hatmaker will notice at once that Artemis and Cerelia would not have the same crown or brim. This is the most likely thing to lose Joseph's trust in a presentation.
3. **The accession numbers are our invention.** JM·H·003 is a design system, not Joseph's. He may already number his hats, or may find the idea alien. It must be offered, not assumed.
4. **The identity is still a typeface, not a mark.** "Joseph *Martin*" in Instrument Serif is a strong setting of a free font, but it is not ownable in the way a drawn wordmark or label would be. Cowboy George is more ownable than anything on the page, and F uses him only inside the club.
5. **The maker page is mostly scaffolding.** Without the interview and photographs, chapters II–IV are honest gaps. They show the structure but cannot move anyone yet.
6. **Some copy is ours, not his.** "Made once, for one head." and "Any colour. Any shape. Yours." are Tangerine lines. They are labelled as study copy, but must be approved or replaced in Joseph's voice.
7. **Ready-to-wear and straw have no presence.** F states that both exist but shows neither, because no data was captured. A third of how people buy is missing from the pictures.
8. **The club's orange is the one exception to the colour rule.** It is defensible, since the club is printed on its own stock, but it is the loudest colour on the homepage and sits in a supporting chapter.
9. **Not yet measured.** Lighthouse, screen-reader passes and a real-device check on a slow network were not run in this iteration.

## What it would take to be presentation-ready for Joseph

In order of impact:

1. **One half-day shoot**, before anything else: the frontispiece (P·001), Joseph at the bench (P·002), the shop wide (P·004), and each of the four hats in profile on plain ground (P·H·001–004). These seven frames turn F from a proposal into something Joseph can recognise himself in. See `docs/PHOTOGRAPHY.md`.
2. **Redraw the hats from the hats.** Trace each silhouette from the profile photographs so every drawing is that hat's actual crown and brim. Keep the annotation system.
3. **The interview** (`docs/CONTENT.md`, about 60 minutes, recorded): one quote for the homepage, a 150–250-word biography, and the stitching answer for the archive. Joseph approves every word.
4. **Confirm the facts:** the AHAB price, one turnaround figure per type of hat, the commission steps, CECH's details, the sizes offered, and whether the posters may be shown as his.
5. **Calibrate the felts** against physical samples in daylight, and replace the estimates.
6. **Decide on the mark:** test the Instrument Serif setting against a drawn wordmark, and against Cowboy George as the house symbol. Bring two options, not one.
7. **Present privately.** This repository is public. Show Joseph a walkthrough on a private preview: five pages, the felt switcher live, E beside F. Bring printed felt swatches and a printed ledger card so the system can be held as well as scrolled.
8. **Then measure:** Lighthouse, VoiceOver and TalkBack passes, and a real phone on a throttled connection.

**This is a study and a recommendation. Nothing here is an approved direction or approved client work.**
