# Reference research

Thirteen references, chosen for **principles** rather than looks. Nothing here is a template: no layout, identity, asset or distinctive system from these is copied into the studies.

> Method note: the working environment's network policy blocked most outside sites, so these observations come from prior study of the references, not from a fresh visit this session. Check them against the live sites before quoting any detail externally.

| # | Reference | Field | What makes it exceptional | Transferable lesson | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anderson & Sheppard** | Bespoke tailoring, London | Explains the bespoke process plainly (fittings, time, price) with no mystique. The relationship is the product. | Say exactly what a commission involves: how many conversations, how long, how much. Confidence is clarity. | A, B, C commission sections |
| 2 | **A. Lange & Söhne** | Watchmaking | Names and explains individual components and finishing steps, so a buyer can see what the price is paying for. | Anatomy as storytelling. Name each part of a hat and show where it appears in a real hat. | B "Anatomy" |
| 3 | **F.P. Journe** | Independent watchmaking | The maker's name is the brand; limited output is stated as fact, not as a marketing scarcity device. | Present the waitlist honestly, as a consequence of one person's hands, never as artificial exclusivity. | All commission copy |
| 4 | **George Nakashima Woodworkers** | American furniture | Each slab is specific; the archive and the living workshop sit side by side; a strong American craft lineage told without costume. | Every material entry should be specific (this felt, this hat), not generic. An American maker without Western clichés. | C archive entries |
| 5 | **Heath Ceramics** | American ceramics | Glaze colours work as a system and a vocabulary customers learn. Factory and process are part of the brand. | Make the material palette (felts) the colour system, with real names. | B felt fields, C swatches |
| 6 | **Margaret Howell** | Clothing, UK | Plain photography, real fabrics, restraint. National character expressed through material and cut, not nostalgia. | "American character without costume": material and proportion over motif. | Palette restraint across all |
| 7 | **MACK** | Photobook publisher | Catalogue-like rigour: consistent plates, captions, metadata. The book as an object. | A product entry can be a plate with a caption, as in a catalogue raisonné, without losing price or availability. | A "The Hats" entries |
| 8 | **Apartamento** | Interiors magazine | Real people photographed in their own lived-in spaces, imperfect and specific. | The People category: customers in their own lives, never styled models. | C people entries, shot list |
| 9 | **Atelier Peter Zumthor** | Architecture | Almost nothing online: a few images, a list of works, material and silence. Absence as authority. | A small site of exceptional quality beats a sprawling one. Cut sections the content can't support. | IA decisions |
| 10 | **The Row** | Fashion | Products on plain ground, minimal type, nothing competing with the object. | Object photography with one light and the shadow kept; no props. | B and A product shot specs |
| 11 | **Kvadrat** | Textiles | Material swatches are primary content, each with a precise name and use. | Felt, ribbon and suede as browsable entries with names and uses. | C materials filter |
| 12 | **Drake's** | Menswear | Editorial photography and shop are woven together; real people, real places; strong voice. | Commerce can sit inside editorial without hiding the price. | C hat entries with price |
| 13 | **Optimo Hats** | American hatmaking | A respected American hat business that documents hand process in detail, which supports its prices. | Process photography is evidence. It is the primary justification for a $1,400 hat. | A "The Hands", shot list |

## Principles taken forward

1. **Evidence over adjectives.** Show the work and explain the decision; never call it "unparalleled".
2. **Material is the system.** Real felt names and colours carry the identity.
3. **Specificity.** This hat, this felt, this charm, this person. No generic luxury.
4. **Clarity in commerce.** Price, time and the way to buy are always one step away.
5. **Restraint scaled to content.** Build only the sections real material can fill.
