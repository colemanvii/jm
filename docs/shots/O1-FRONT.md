# Shot O1 · Western hat, front view — framed to Fig. 01

**Purpose.** The homepage opens on Fig. 01, the line drawing of a western hat, front view. When a visitor taps **See the hats**, the hats page should open on a photograph of a real Joseph Martin hat in exactly the same place on screen: crown centre on the same line, brim tips and front edge of the brim at the same width and height. A straight cut, so the eye stays on the same curve as the drawing becomes the hat.

**Status (24 Nov 2026).** No photograph in the project matches. The only real hats in any asset are in the workshop portrait: Joseph's black hat is worn and tilted, and the tan hat is cut off at the frame edge and seen from above at about 200 px. Until this shot exists, See the hats keeps its current destination.

## Files

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `O1-overlay-3x2.png` | 3000 × 2000 transparent overlay, magenta lines. Load it over the live view when tethered (Capture One: *Overlay* tool; Lightroom: *Loupe Overlay → Layout Image*). |
| `O1-reference-3x2.png` | The same framing on the tobacco field, for the call sheet. |

Both are drawn from Fig. 01’s own paths, so the overlay is the drawing.

## The frame

- **Orientation:** landscape, 3:2, uncropped.
- **Brim:** tip to tip spans **40% of the frame width**, between the two vertical guides.
- **Crown:** its centre (the bottom of the crease) sits on the **centre line**.
- **Height:** brim tips on the upper horizontal guide, front edge of the brim on the lower one, top of crown on the top guide.
- **Margins:** the two dotted boxes are the crops the site will use: the full desktop screen (1440 × 900) and the top 420 px of a phone screen (390 wide). The phone crop uses nearly the full frame height, so **keep the hat exactly where the overlay puts it**.

## Camera

- Full-frame body, 45 MP or more. 100–135 mm lens, about 3–4 m from the hat, f/11, ISO 100, tripod.
- Lens at the **height of the band**, sensor plane vertical (check with a level: no tilt up or down), camera centred on the crown’s centre line. This keeps the brim reading as the drawing’s shallow sweep and stops the crown from leaning.

## The hat

- One finished hat whose shape is closest to Fig. 01: a pinched crown with a centre crease, brim turned up at the sides. **Joseph to choose.**
- Recommendation: a **natural**-felt hat (as AHAB). Cream felt on a tobacco ground repeats the drawing’s cream line on tobacco, so the cut reads as the line becoming the object. A **whiskey** hat (ORIS) on the same ground is the tonal alternative. Optional: repeat the frame, unchanged, with each of the four felts.
- Steam and brush the hat first. Band and bow as sold.
- Support it from behind with a clear acrylic stand hidden by the crown, or from above with monofilament. Retouching only removes the support.

## Ground and light

- Seamless paper as close as possible to the site’s tobacco field, **#8E5A2C**, lit **evenly**: no gradient or vignette in camera. Also shoot one frame of the empty set.
- One large soft source high and to camera left, as in the workshop; a white fill card to camera right; a small backlight only if the brim edge disappears into the ground. Keep the brim’s natural shadow under the crown.
- Shoot a grey card and colour checker in the hat’s position under the same light.

## Delivery

- RAW and 16-bit TIFF, **uncropped**, neutral white balance from the grey card, no grading, grain or filters.
- Name the hat and felt in the file name, for example `O1-front_AHAB_natural.tif`.
- Photographer’s name and the credit wording Joseph wants.

## How the site uses it (for the build)

The hats page opens with the photograph full bleed under the same header, scaled and positioned so that:

| Screen | Brim tip to tip | Crown centre x | Front edge of brim, from top |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop 1440 × 900 | 804 px | 720 px | 536 px |
| Phone 390 wide | 277 px | 195 px | 243 px |

These are Fig. 01’s measured positions on the homepage, so the cut lands on the same curve. The drawing itself does not change.
